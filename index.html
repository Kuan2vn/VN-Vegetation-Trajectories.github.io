FROM THE EXAMPLE TO THE RESEARCH QUESTION

Detect the loss. Then check the outcome.

A before–after image pair tells us where vegetation disappeared. It leaves another question open: does that area remain without vegetation, or become green again? The Yellowstone sequence makes the need for a later observation visible. The same follow-up question also arises after land clearing and other changes in vegetation cover.

Our proposed dataset brings this question to Vietnamese landscapes: map the original loss footprint, then label its observed outcome at a defined follow-up. AI models would learn to produce that spatial summary, helping analysts prioritize events for review and compare their outcomes.

We measure visible vegetation cover. Green cover returning does not by itself establish forest restoration, ecological recovery, or the cause of a disturbance. Our initial design targets a season-aware follow-up of roughly one year, rather than the decades shown above.

Example use case

After mapping newly cleared areas around a growing city, an analyst revisits them at a comparable season. Which areas remain without vegetation, and which have green cover again? The benchmark is designed to test whether AI can produce that spatial summary.

THE AI TASK

Three observations in. One outcome map out.

The proposed task is temporal change segmentation: assign a label to each reviewed pixel using a sequence of co-registered multispectral images.

INPUT

Sentinel-2 at three time points

  1. t₀ · BeforeVegetation is present before the target loss event.
  2. t₁ · After lossVegetation cover has been removed or substantially reduced.
  3. t₂ · Follow-upA later observation establishes the visible outcome.

A roughly one-year, season-aware follow-up is the initial design target. Exact timing and cover thresholds will be fixed in the annotation protocol.

OUTPUT

A pixel-level trajectory map

Schematic only. The loss footprint is the union of persistent-loss and regreened pixels with a known outcome.

0

No target loss

No qualifying vegetation-loss event in the reviewed interval. This includes stable vegetation and stable non-vegetated areas.

1

Persistent loss

Vegetation was lost between t₀ and t₁, and cover remains reduced at the specified follow-up.

2

Regreened

Vegetation was lost between t₀ and t₁, and green cover is present again at follow-up.

255

Ignore / uncertain

Cloud, shadow, mixed boundaries, or insufficient evidence prevent a reliable decision. Excluded from supervised scoring.

Three target classes plus an ignore value—not four semantic classes. “Persistent” refers to the selected follow-up, not permanence. A regreened area may contain crops, grass, or young vegetation; it is not automatically a restored forest.

01 / CURATE

Find interpretable events

Select clear observations and check seasonal context. Routine crop cycles and ambiguous changes need explicit handling before inclusion.

02 / ANNOTATE

Review the loss and follow-up

Draw spatial masks from image evidence, retain uncertainty, and review difficult examples. Spectral indices assist annotation; they are not reference labels by themselves.

03 / BENCHMARK

Test segmentation models

Compare simple spectral baselines and temporal segmentation models. Keep geographic groups separate to test transfer beyond nearby training patches.

MULTISPECTRAL

More than a photograph

Visible and near-infrared bands are available at 10 m. Red-edge and shortwave-infrared bands add vegetation and moisture information at native 20 m resolution.

PRACTICAL SCALE

Open data, repeat observations

Sentinel-2 supports reproducible collection without a field campaign. The initial scope targets changes large enough to interpret at its spatial resolution.

OBSERVATION LIMITS

Not every pixel is clear

A 10 m pixel covers about 100 m². Small trees, narrow strips, cloud, haze, and mixed pixels complicate labels. Resampling a 20 m band does not create 10 m detail.

POSITIONING THE CONTRIBUTION

A focused extension of existing research

Temporal land-cover mapping and vegetation change detection already exist. Our proposed contribution is reviewed event footprints with explicit follow-up outcomes in Vietnamese landscapes.

ResourceWhat it providesHow our proposed task relates
DynamicEarthNetSatellite time series and monthly dense land-cover masks.Some loss/regreening trajectories can be derived from its semantic labels. We instead target event-centered cover-loss and follow-up masks directly.
TERRA-CDSentinel-2 image pairs with vegetation increase, decrease, and no-change labels.Strong overlap with vegetation change mapping. Our three-observation design asks what happens after a confirmed loss, rather than only the net change between two dates.
OlmoEarth LCCLand-cover change reference points, intervals, and transition categories; includes Vietnamese examples.A possible event-discovery resource. Dense footprints and independently reviewed post-loss outcomes would require additional annotation.

This is a scoped comparison, not a claim that loss–regreening trajectories are new or absent elsewhere. The proposed benchmark’s value must come from useful event coverage, trustworthy annotations, and reproducible evaluation. Dataset reuse and redistribution depend on the relevant source terms.
