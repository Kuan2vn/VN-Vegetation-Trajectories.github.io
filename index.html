PROPOSED DATASET AND AI TASK

Event-centered vegetation-loss and outcome segmentation

Input: three co-registered Sentinel-2 observations before loss, after loss, and at follow-up. Output: a pixel-level map of persistent loss, regreening, or no target loss, with uncertain pixels excluded.

Label definitions and annotation considerations
0 No target loss
No qualifying vegetation-loss event in the reviewed interval, including stable vegetation and stable non-vegetated areas.
1 Persistent loss
Vegetation was lost between the first two observations, and cover remains reduced at the specified follow-up.
2 Regreened
Vegetation was lost between the first two observations, and green cover is present again at follow-up.
255 Ignore / uncertain
Cloud, shadow, mixed boundaries, or insufficient evidence prevent a reliable decision. These pixels are excluded from supervised scoring.

Three target classes plus an ignore value. “Persistent” refers to the selected follow-up, not permanence; regreening describes visible cover, not ecological restoration. The loss footprint is the union of classes 1 and 2 where the outcome is known.

The annotation protocol will specify cover thresholds, minimum event size, seasonal checks, crop-cycle handling, and uncertainty rules. Spectral indices will assist review rather than serve as reference labels on their own.

Planned reference data and evaluation

01 / LOSS FOOTPRINTS

Reviewed spatial masks

Dense masks identifying selected vegetation-loss events from the available image evidence.

02 / FOLLOW-UP OUTCOMES

Cover status after loss

Outcome labels within the loss footprint, with the follow-up interval and annotation uncertainty documented.

03 / MODEL EVALUATION

A documented benchmark protocol

Geographic splits and spectral and temporal segmentation baselines to evaluate performance on the proposed labels.

These are planned outputs. Temporal vegetation change is an established research task, and some trajectories can be derived from existing semantic labels. The proposed contribution is a reviewed event-focused reference set and evaluation protocol for selected Vietnamese landscapes.

Spatial resolution, spectral information, and interpretation limits
MULTISPECTRAL

Spectral information

Visible and near-infrared bands are available at 10 m. Red-edge and shortwave-infrared bands add vegetation and moisture information at native 20 m resolution.

PRACTICAL SCALE

Repeat observations

Sentinel-2 supports reproducible collection without a field campaign. The initial scope targets changes large enough to interpret at its spatial resolution.

OBSERVATION LIMITS

Spatial and atmospheric limits

A 10 m pixel covers about 100 m². Small trees, narrow strips, cloud, haze, and mixed pixels complicate labels. Resampling a 20 m band does not create 10 m detail.

RELATED DATASETS

Relationship to existing reference data

Temporal land-cover mapping and vegetation change detection already exist. We plan to annotate event footprints and follow-up cover outcomes for selected Vietnamese sites.

ResourceWhat it providesHow our proposed task relates
DynamicEarthNetSatellite time series and monthly dense land-cover masks.Some loss/regreening trajectories can be derived from its semantic labels. We plan to annotate event-centered cover loss and follow-up outcomes directly.
TERRA-CDSentinel-2 image pairs with vegetation increase, decrease, and no-change labels.Overlaps with vegetation change mapping. Our three-observation design asks what happens after a confirmed loss, rather than only the net change between two dates.
OlmoEarth LCCLand-cover change reference points, intervals, and transition categories; includes Vietnamese examples.A possible event-discovery resource. Dense footprints and independently reviewed post-loss outcomes would require additional annotation.

This is a scoped comparison, not a claim that loss–regreening trajectories are new or absent elsewhere. The proposed benchmark’s value must come from useful event coverage, trustworthy annotations, and reproducible evaluation. Dataset reuse and redistribution depend on the relevant source terms.
